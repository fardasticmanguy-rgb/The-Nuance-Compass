Politics is not agree or disagree.

Most compass tests ask whether you agree. Almost nobody simply does. You agree up to a point, or only if, or you would agree if the question meant the thing you think it means. This one lets you say which.

Takes about 15 minutes. Nothing leaves your browser.

Conditions, not absolutes

Every statement carries the clauses people actually add: only if, unless, but not when. Pick the ones you mean and they change your score.

No jargon

Every question has an explain button covering what it is really asking and what each side tends to imply.

Six axes

Two axes cannot hold a person. The classic compass, plus culture, borders, ecology and who you trust to decide.

Answer how you like

Five plain choices by default. If you want to place yourself between two of them, open the fine-tune scale.

No score until the end

The test does not show you where you are landing while you answer, because a result you can see is a result you start aiming at.

How the scoring works

Each statement pulls on one or more axes. Your slider position sets how hard it pulls, and the weighting you give a question multiplies it. Conditions attach their own smaller pulls, which is how agree, but only with a warrant lands somewhere different from a flat agree.

Each axis is scored against the strongest possible answer to the questions you actually answered, so skipping questions does not distort the rest. The statements are polarity balanced: agreeing with all one hundred puts you within a point or two of dead centre, so the test cannot be passed by nodding along.

About The Nuance Compass

What is The Nuance Compass?

The Nuance Compass is a free political compass test of 100 statements scored across six axes. Instead of agree or disagree, you answer on a scale and attach the conditions you actually mean, so a qualified agreement scores differently from a flat one.

How long does the test take?

The full test is 100 questions and takes about 15 minutes. The short version is 30 questions and takes about 5 minutes. Your progress is saved as you go, so you can stop and pick it up later, or carry it to another device with a link.

Which axes does it measure?

Six: economics, authority, culture, borders, ecology, and who you trust to decide. The first two are the classic left-right and libertarian-authoritarian axes of the political compass. The other four cover ground two axes cannot hold.

Is it free, and does it collect my answers?

It is free, with no account and no sign-up. The test runs entirely in your browser, your answers are stored only on your own device, and nothing is sent to a server.
