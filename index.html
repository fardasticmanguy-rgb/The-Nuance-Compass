The Nuance Compass

One hundred questions · Six axes · No boxes to tick

Politics is not agree or disagree.

Every compass test asks whether you agree. Almost nobody simply does. You agree up to a point, or you agree only if, or you would agree if the question meant the thing you think it means. This one is built around that.

Takes about 15 minutes. Nothing leaves your browser.

A slider, not a box

Answer anywhere on a continuous scale. Mild agreement and total conviction are not the same thing, and they are not scored the same way.

Conditions, not absolutes

Every statement carries the clauses people actually add: only if, unless, but not when. Pick the ones you mean and they change your score.

Nothing is jargon

Every question has an i explaining what it is really asking and what each side tends to imply. No assumed vocabulary.

Six axes, one map

Two axes cannot hold a person. You get the classic compass plus culture, borders, ecology and who you trust to decide.

How the scoring works

Each statement pulls on one or more axes. Your slider position sets how hard it pulls, and the weighting you give a question multiplies it. Conditions attach their own smaller pulls, which is how agree, but only with a warrant lands somewhere different from a flat agree.

Each axis is scored against the strongest possible answer to the questions you actually answered, so skipping questions does not distort the rest. The statements are polarity balanced: agreeing with all one hundred puts you within a point or two of dead centre, so the test cannot be passed by nodding along.
